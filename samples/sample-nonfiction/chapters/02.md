# The Ledger

The ledger is a single volume, calf-bound, 340 pages, of which 212 are used. It
was deposited with the county archive in 1954 by a solicitor acting for a
family that no longer owned the land the road ran across.

It records payments. That is what a ledger does, and it is easy to be
disappointed by it — there is no narrative in a payment, no description of the
weather or the argument at the parish meeting. What there is, is a sequence: who
was paid, for what, on which day. Read in order, over five years, the sequence
becomes a story, and the story is not the one the secondary sources tell.

## What the ledger shows

Three things, which this chapter works through in turn.

The first is that the work stopped in the winter of 1823 and did not resume
until the spring of 1824 — a gap the secondary sources describe as "weather"
and the ledger describes as an absence of payments to anyone at all, including
the men who would have been paid to do nothing.
