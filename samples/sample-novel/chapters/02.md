## Chapter 2

The letter had been in the drawer for six weeks.

Marta had found it in March, in the box of their mother's papers that nobody
had been ready to open, and she had read it standing up and then put it back
and closed the drawer and gone downstairs and made tea. She had not told
anyone. She had not decided not to tell anyone; she had simply not told
anyone, which is a different thing and, she suspected, a worse one.

The handwriting was not her mother's.

That was the fact she kept arriving at, the way a tongue arrives at a chipped
tooth. Everything else about the letter could be explained. The date could be
wrong. The paper could be older than it looked. The signature could be a
maiden name, a joke, a mistake. But the hand was a stranger's, and their mother
had kept it for forty years in a drawer with the things she meant to keep.
